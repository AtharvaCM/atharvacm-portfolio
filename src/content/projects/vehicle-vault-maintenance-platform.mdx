## Overview

Vehicle Vault is an India-focused ownership platform for cars and two-wheelers: one place for what a vehicle has had done, what it is covered by, what it costs, and what is due next. It started as a maintenance log. It is now a domain model with its own vocabulary, an alert engine, a phone-first app that opens on what needs doing, and a public catalog that lets anyone look up their vehicle before they sign up.

## The ownership model

Most of the interesting work is in refusing to flatten things that behave differently. A **vehicle document** is a time-bounded paper (insurance, warranty, registration, PUC, road tax) unified behind one service with per-kind adapters, and each paper drives its own renewal reminder. A **maintenance record** is a service visit with line items, costs, and an odometer reading, which is why it is not a document. An **accessory** is a purchase with a fitment lifecycle, kept out of maintenance so it cannot corrupt the cost-per-kilometre figure that maintenance history exists to produce. Tyres are tracked by measurement rather than inferred, with front and rear positions, a two-wheel layout, and a verdict on each.

On top of that sit loans with amortization, prepayments, and foreclosure; claims linked to both a document and the repair that settled them; and shared vehicle access with owner, editor, and viewer roles, invites, and ownership transfer, for the household where one person maintains everyone's vehicles.

## Knowing what is due

The alert engine is a daily cron that reads each vehicle's predicted odometer and raises typed alerts: maintenance and reminders due or overdue, papers expiring, accessory warranties running out, a warranty nearing its distance limit, tyres worn, aged, or unmeasured for too long, and service baselines that were never established. Each kind owns its own template and dedup key, so a notification row is upserted rather than duplicated, and delivery fans out to email and web push by the owner's per-kind preferences without either channel being able to break the record.

Baselines are the quiet part. A vehicle entering the vault has history nobody logged, so it carries a per-category baseline that is either a known figure or an explicit *unknown*. An unmeasured tyre is treated as unknown rather than healthy, because that is a thing worth saying out loud.

## Phone first

The app was rebuilt around five places: **Home**, a to-do list with one status line; **Garage**, a list of vehicles led by the number plate; **Upcoming**, one timeline across every vehicle; **History**, service, fuel, and odometer by month; and **Costs**, spend and loans together. A vehicle page is an identity header and five tabs that fit a 390 px phone without scrolling.

Logging is built for the forecourt. Snap the bill and Gemini-backed extraction reads it into a draft, so the form opens filled in, with each field it read marked as coming from the bill. The draft counts toward no cost, report, or reminder until someone confirms it. A reminder's Done hands off to logging the service, and the service completes the reminder and schedules the next one. The whole thing sits on a design system of semantic tokens with self-hosted fonts, dark mode, bottom sheets on phones, and lint rules that fail a migrated screen for reaching past the tokens. It installs as an app and opens offline.

## The public catalog

Vehicle entry is backed by a curated India catalog: staged imports reviewed in-app before publishing, curated aliases so messy real-world labels like "Old Swift ZXI" still resolve, and generation and year-aware offerings. That catalog is now public. Make, model, and variant pages for more than thirty brands of cars and bikes are prerendered to static HTML with breadcrumbs, JSON-LD, and a sitemap, behind a page-quality gate that keeps thin pages out of the index. A variant page leads with key facts, its service schedule, and a running-cost calculator, and "track this vehicle" carries that intent through sign-up so a new account lands on the add-vehicle form with it already filled in.

## Delivery

The repo is a pnpm workspace with `apps/web`, `apps/api`, and shared contract and config packages, on conventional commits with semantic-release cutting versions and the changelog. CI runs typecheck, lint, Prettier, Vitest units, and Playwright coverage of the core garage journey, plus axe checks across the public pages and the signed-in app, and a gate that fails the build when a public table ships without RLS enabled. The frontend deploys to Vercel; the API publishes as a GHCR image to a self-hosted Portainer stack and applies its own Prisma migrations on container start.
